Computer Networks & Data Communication

Study Guide & Reference Material

1. Introduction to Computer Networks & LAN

A computer network is a system where two or more computing devices are interconnected to share data, resources, and information. These devices, known as nodes, communicate via wired or wireless media to enable resource sharing, efficient communication, and centralized management.

Local Area Network (LAN)

Connects devices within a small geographical area (home, office, campus).

  • Covers a limited area
  • High data transfer rates (up to 10 Gbps)
  • Typically owned and managed by a single organization

2. Characteristics & Components of Data Communication

Four Key Characteristics:

  • Delivery: Data must reach the intended destination only.
  • Accuracy: Data must be delivered without transmission errors.
  • Timeliness: Data must arrive promptly without significant delays.
  • Jitter: Inconsistent packet arrival intervals must be minimized.

Data Flow Modes:

  • Simplex: Unidirectional transmission (e.g., keyboard to CPU).
  • Half-Duplex: Both devices transmit, but only one at a time (e.g., walkie-talkie).
  • Full-Duplex: Simultaneous two-way transmission (e.g., telephone call).

3. Network Topologies

  • Mesh Topology: Every device connects directly to every other device via $n(n-1)/2$ links. Provides dedicated channels and high fault tolerance, but has high cabling costs.
  • Star Topology: All devices connect to a central hub/switch. Simple to configure; hub failure brings down the network.
  • Bus Topology: Nodes share a single backbone cable. Cost-effective, but cable faults disrupt all devices.
  • Ring Topology: Nodes form a closed loop; tokens pass sequentially through repeaters.

4. Transmission Media

Twisted-Pair Cable

Composed of two insulated copper conductors twisted around each other to cancel out electromagnetic interference and crosstalk.

  • UTP (Unshielded Twisted-Pair): Standard, flexible, low-cost (uses RJ-45 connector).
  • STP (Shielded Twisted-Pair): Includes protective metallic foil shielding against noise.

Coaxial Cable

Features a central core conductor, inner dielectric insulator, braided outer shield, and protective outer jacket. Terminated with BNC connectors (BNC, BNC T, BNC Terminator).
